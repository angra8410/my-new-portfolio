5 key phases of Colombian labor market
Phase 1 — Recovery (2004–2010)
Unemployment fell from 14.3% to 11.8% under Uribe. Strong commodity boom and FDI drove job creation. PEA grew steadily.
Phase 2 — Golden decade (2010–2018)
Santos era saw unemployment drop to historic lows near 7.8% (Oct 2013). Peace accord 2016 opened new economic zones. Employed population hit ~23M.
Phase 3 — COVID shock (2020)
Unemployment exploded to 20.9% in Jul 2020. April 2020 lockdown collapsed formal employment to 850K. DANE survey methodology broke down.
Phase 4 — Rebound (2021–2022)
Fastest post-crisis recovery in Colombia's history. PEA grew 28% in 2021. Unemployment back below 12% by end of 2021.
Phase 5 — New normal (2023–2026)
Unemployment reached series low of 7.0% in Nov 2025. PEA surpassed 26.5M. Petro-era social programs and formal employment push showing results.
key analytical insights
Colombia has been on a structural downtrend in unemployment since 2004 — dropping roughly 7 percentage points over 20 years, from 14.3% to 7.0%, interrupted only by COVID.
The 2021 data (Jan–Dec) shows suspiciously round values (17%, 15.9%, 15.1%...) — these appear to be estimated or interpolated figures, not raw DANE survey data. The Gold table may need a data quality review for 2021.
2006 shows abnormally high employed values (~54M) vs adjacent years (~17M) — clear data anomaly from the SUM fanout issue (duplicated rows in Gold layer for that period).
The PEA has grown from ~28M in 2004 to 26.5M in 2026. The apparent decrease is likely a methodology change — DANE revised survey coverage in 2019, so pre-2019 and post-2019 PEA figures are not directly comparable.
